Millbrook Rolls Out Adaptive Traffic Signals Downtown

The city of Millbrook has begun switching on a network of adaptive traffic signals
across its downtown core, replacing fixed-timer lights at 14 intersections. The new
signals use roadway sensors to adjust green-light timing based on real-time traffic
volume, and city engineers say the system should trim average weekday commute times
through downtown by several minutes.

The rollout follows an eight-month pilot on Harding Avenue, where the city reported
smoother traffic flow during peak hours and shorter queues at side streets. The
signals also give buses a few extra seconds of green when they are running behind
schedule, part of the city's push to make its transit routes more reliable.

Not everyone is convinced. Some downtown merchants worry the retimed lights will
speed cars past their storefronts, and a residents' association has asked the city
to publish the system's traffic data so the public can verify the promised gains.
The city council has scheduled a review of the program for early next year.
