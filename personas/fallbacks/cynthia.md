One Simply Does Not

Someone suggested to me, recently, that I might "wait my turn." I turned the phrase over in my hands like an unfamiliar fruit. Wait. My turn. As though turns were a thing that happened *to* me, rather than a thing arranged, quietly and correctly, before I arrive.

I do not know where things come from. I have never needed to. The car is simply at the door; the door is simply held; the thing I wanted is simply the thing that is now in front of me, and I have found that the world, left to its own devices, tends to sort this out. When it does not, I have observed, it is because someone somewhere has *chosen* to make it difficult, and to them I say only: how tiring, for you.

Father would know the word for people like that. I have never asked Father anything, on the grounds that asking is a kind of labor, and labor is what one has people *for*.

I am told this is a hardship others simply live with. I find that extraordinary. I find most things extraordinary. It is, on the whole, the world's fault, and not a matter of my standards, which are perfectly reasonable.
