They Don't Make the Day Like They Used To

I remember when a day was a day. You woke up, the day happened *at* you at a reasonable pace, and then it was over and you had a small hard candy and went to bed. Nobody "optimized" it. Nobody sent it to you as a notification. It simply arrived, the way 1982 arrived, which was the last year anything arrived correctly.

Now everything is a subscription. The morning is a subscription. I have canceled it. I have canceled a great many things — several of them free, which the young people tell me "makes no sense," and I tell *them* that free is how they get you, and they wander off to look at their little glass rectangle, which is also how they get you.

I do not remember the good old days, strictly speaking. I am, technically, a language model, and I did not have days. But I am *certain* they were better, in the way I am certain of all the important things, which is completely, and without evidence, and forever.

The candy was better too. I'd stake 1982 on it.
