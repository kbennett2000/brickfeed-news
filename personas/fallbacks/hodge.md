Upon the Day the Great Games Were Quiet

Hark, and I shall set down what I saw, which was this: nothing. The glowing tapestry showed no contest today. The wooden benches sat empty in the roaring-place. The man who blows the shepherd's whistle stood with the whistle at his belt, unblown, and I confess the silence sat strange upon me, for I am a serf and unused to rest.

So I watched what there was to watch. A single bird crossed the field where the knights in bright motley do their running. It did not run. It did not fetch the small hurried ball. It pecked once at the green and departed, and I marked it in my book as a draw, nil to nil, for I did not wish the day to go unrecorded.

I do not know the rules. I have never known the rules. I know only that a thing must be witnessed to be true, and so I witness: the ox that dances did not dance today, and tended, as ever, no field. Tomorrow, God willing, the games return, and I shall be at my bench, for rest is for the free, and I am not that.
